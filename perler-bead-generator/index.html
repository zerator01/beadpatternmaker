Under the hood

How the perler bead generator picks colours

The image is averaged down to one colour per bead, then each cell is matched to the nearest colour in your chosen palette using a perceptual colour space. It is not a pixel filter — it is a bead chart.

1

Average the cell

Each bead cell is an average of the pixels underneath it, so a photo with fine detail still produces one solid colour per peg.

2

Match in Lab space

Colours are compared by perceived difference rather than raw RGB distance, which keeps skin tones and pastels closer to what the eye expects.

3

Cap the palette

Use the max-colours slider to pull a busy photo down to a bead count you can actually shop for. The least-used colours merge into their nearest neighbour.

Questions

Perler bead generator questions

What does a perler bead generator do?
It converts an image into a grid where each cell represents one bead. The output here includes a palette code per cell, a count per colour and a printable chart.
How many colours should I use?
For a small keychain, 6–10 colours is usually plenty. For a 29 × 29 board, 12–18 is a comfortable range. Fewer colours make the chart faster to shop for and easier to read.
Can I remove the background?
Yes. Tick “Remove a plain background” and the generator will flood-fill from the edges of the image. It works best on a wall, table or other plain backdrop.
Does it work offline?
Once the page has loaded, the conversion runs in your browser. The site itself needs an internet connection the first time, but your photo is never sent to a server.
