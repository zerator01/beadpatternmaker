The export

The part most pattern makers skip: the bead list

A chart tells you where the beads go. It does not tell you that you need 47 white, 31 pink and 9 yellow before you sit down — so you find out halfway through a piece.

Every pattern you make here ships with a count per colour, sorted by how many you need. The PDF puts it on its own page so you can take it to the craft shop, and the CSV export lets you check the list against the beads already in your stash.

Colour values are screen approximations. Bead batches vary, so always eyeball a few of your own beads against the swatches before you commit to a big piece.

Sizing

Which pegboard should I use?

A 29 × 29 square pegboard is the workhorse for perler projects. It gives you enough cells for a clear animal face or a small scene without turning into a multi-evening build.

Use a 10 × 10 or 16 × 16 board for keychains and magnets. Go to 48 × 48 or 64 × 64 when you want a wall piece and are ready to print the chart across several sheets.

Questions

Questions people ask before their first board

Is this perler bead pattern maker free?
Yes. The maker, the coded chart, the bead counts, the PNG and the print-ready PDF are free, with no account and no watermark. The photo is processed in your browser and is never uploaded to a server.
What kind of photo makes the best perler bead pattern?
Simple, high-contrast images with one clear subject work best. Crop close to the subject, avoid busy backgrounds, and choose a grid size that leaves enough cells for the details you care about. Fewer cells usually makes a bolder, more readable piece.
Which bead palettes can I use?
The maker includes Perler-style, Hama-style and Artkal-style palettes. Switch palettes before you export and the chart is remapped to the nearest colour in that set. Colour values are screen approximations, so compare a real bead with the swatch before a large project.
How many beads will my pattern need?
The chart counts every filled cell and breaks the total down by colour. The same list is on the last page of the PDF and can be downloaded as a CSV, so you can check it against the beads already in your stash.
How do I print the pattern?
Download the PDF and print at 100% on A4 or Letter paper. Large grids are tiled across several sheets with the column and row range printed on each one. Every fifth line is heavier so you can count rows without losing your place.
Can I edit the pattern after it is generated?
Yes. Click any bead to paint it, erase it, or pick up its colour. Undo and redo cover every stroke, and the bead counts update as you edit. That matters most around eyes, outlines and small details where an automatic conversion needs a correction.
