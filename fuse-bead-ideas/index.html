Fuse bead ideas

Fuse bead ideas for the moment you have a tray of beads and no plan. This page is sorted by what you want to make — easy first projects, small pieces for keychains and magnets, gifts and cards, party and holiday decorations, things for your room, and 3D builds. Every idea comes with a ready-made design, an exact bead count and a free printable chart, plus a tip for turning the flat piece into something you will actually use. Fuse beads, Perler beads and Hama beads are the same craft under different brand names, so each idea works with whichever jar you own. Six ways to finish a design

What can you make with fuse beads?

The chart is only half the project. A finished piece needs a backing, a loop or a reason to sit on a shelf.

✦

Keychain

Add a jump ring and a split ring. Use a 10 × 10 or 16 × 16 design so it does not pull at a pocket.

✦

Fridge magnet

Glue a magnetic strip to the back. A 13 × 13 design is light enough to stay put.

✦

Coaster

Centre a simple motif on a 29 × 29 board, fill the space around it and back it with cork. Iron both sides well.

✦

Pin badge

Use a small design and attach a pin back with strong glue. Avoid ironing the back too flat where the pin sits.

✦

Bag charm

Make two matching designs, iron them flat, and join with a short chain or a bead loop.

✦

Ornament

Leave a gap at the top for a ribbon, or add a loop of beads before fusing. Good for holiday trees and windows.

Naming

Fuse beads, perler beads, hama beads — same idea?

They are all small plastic beads with a hole through the middle that are placed on a pegboard and fused with heat. “Perler” is the best-known brand in the US, “Hama” is common in Europe, and “Artkal” is popular for large colour ranges.

Because the bead size and grid are the same, a design written for one can usually be made with another. The colours will not match exactly — that is why the maker lets you switch palettes and see the closest available bead.

If you are shopping for beads, check the bead diameter on the packet. Midi beads are about 5 mm and fit the standard square pegboards; mini beads need finer tips and a different board.

Before you start

  • ✓Match the bead size to your pegboard
  • ✓Count the colours before you sit down
  • ✓Keep a spare sheet of ironing paper handy
  • ✓Work on a flat, heat-safe surface
  • ✓Let the piece cool under a book to stop curling
